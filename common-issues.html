Common Issues

The snags we find again and again

No two properties are the same, but the same kinds of defects turn up in new homes across Munster and Connacht. Most are small. Together, they are the difference between a home that was finished properly and one that was just finished.

Paint & Plaster

Hairline cracks in fresh plaster, rough or uneven finishes, missed patches, paint on hinges, handles and glass, and walls marked during the final fit-out.

Doors

Doors that stick, swing or fail to latch, misaligned frames, damaged handles and ironmongery, and gaps that let draughts through.

Windows

Scratched glass, damaged frames, stiff or faulty openers, broken seals and trickle vents left closed, blocked or painted over.

Kitchen

Damaged or misaligned unit doors, worktops with chips or poor joints, appliances not fitted or connected properly, and leaking traps under the sink.

Bathrooms

Gaps in grout and sealant, loose taps and fittings, slow drainage, toilet seats and cisterns not fitted right, and extractor fans that do not run.

Floors & Stairs

Uneven floor levels, squeaks and movement underfoot, damaged boards or tiles, loose stair handrails and uneven steps.

Electrics

Sockets, switches and lights tested throughout; dead points, loose fittings and extractor or cooker circuits that were never connected.

Heating & Plumbing

Radiators that do not heat evenly or leak at the valves, dripping taps, low water pressure and pipework left unlagged or unfinished.

Outside

Cracked or stained render, blocked gullies and downpipes, paving and driveways unfinished, and fencing, gates and gardens left incomplete.

Why so many?

A new home is finished by a long line of trades working to a deadline, often in poor weather and poorer light. Small defects are almost inevitable. The problem is not that snags exist; it is moving in without a record of them.

Every one in writing

Each snag found is photographed and written into the snag list with its location in the property, so the builder cannot argue about what was found or where.
