Buying a new home? Get it snagged before you sign.

A snagging inspection finds the defects, unfinished work and poor finishes in a new build, and puts them in a written list your builder has to work through.

Almost every new home is handed over with snags, from cracked plaster and sticking doors to leaks and dead sockets. Find them before you close and the builder puts them right at their cost. Find them after you move in and they are your problem.

The Short Version

Small defects, found while they are still the builder's problem

A snag is anything in a new home that is damaged, unfinished or not fitted properly. Most are minor on their own. The point of a snagging inspection is to catch all of them in one visit, in writing, while the builder is still obliged to fix them.

Found

A room-by-room inspection of the whole property, inside and out, checking finishes, fittings and how everything works.

Written Up

Every snag is photographed and listed clearly, room by room, in a report you can send straight to the builder or developer.

Fixed

The builder works through the list and puts the snags right before you close, or in an agreed round of repairs afterwards.

How It Works

Three steps, all before you get the keys

01

Book once the builder says it is finished

The right time is when the builder tells you the home is complete and the water and electricity are on, and before you close the sale. Get in touch for a quote with the property details and your closing date.

02

Room-by-room inspection

The property is inspected inside and out, room by room, working through a full checklist. Every defect found is noted and photographed.

03

Snag list to the builder

You get a written snag list with photographs to hand to the builder. They work through it, and a re-inspection can be arranged to check the repairs.
