What Is Snagging

What a snagging inspection is, and why it matters

Snagging is a detailed inspection of a newly built or newly finished property, carried out before you take full possession. It produces a snag list: a written record of every defect, unfinished item and poor finish, for the builder to put right.

Why It Matters

Before you close, the builder pays. After, you do.

New homes are built quickly, by many different trades, and even good builders hand over properties with defects. Some are cosmetic, like paint and plaster. Others, like leaks, faulty seals and doors that do not close, get worse and costlier the longer they are left.

Caught before handover

Snags recorded before you close go back to the builder as a list to work through. The repairs are done at the builder's cost, in the builder's time, before your furniture is in the way.

Found after you move in

The same defects found later mean chasing the builder yourself, arranging trades around your own schedule, and paying out of your own pocket for anything that is argued over or left too long.

What it is not

A snag list is not a structural survey. It records the defects and unfinished work that can be seen and tested on the day. It does not certify that the property complies with the Building Regulations; that sign-off sits with the Assigned Certifier under the Building Control Regulations.

Costs vs benefits

The inspection itself is a fixed, quoted cost agreed before the visit. What it buys is a complete, photographed record of everything wrong with the property at the one moment the builder is obliged to fix it for free. Against the cost of putting even a handful of defects right yourself, it is money well spent.

When To Inspect

The right time to book

01

When the builder says the home is finished

Book once the builder or developer confirms the property is complete, with water and electricity connected and working, so everything can be tested properly.

02

Before you close the sale

The inspection and the snag list need to happen before you sign off and draw down the final payment. That is when you have the most leverage to get the list worked through.

03

Again after the repairs

Once the builder has worked through the list, a re-inspection (sometimes called a de-snag) checks that the repairs have actually been done and done properly.
